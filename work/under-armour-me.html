The problem

Under Armour ME needed to behave like a modern retail app, not a catalogue with a checkout attached. That meant personalised recommendations, a reason to open the app inside a store, and regional payment options — while marketing changed campaigns far more often than the App Store review cycle allows.

The app also had to report the same conversions consistently to Branch, MoEngage and Google Analytics 4 — three SDKs with three different event vocabularies.

What I built

  • Personalised product recommendations and QR scanning.
  • Location-based in-store experiences.
  • An analytics layer capturing 20+ conversion events across Branch, MoEngage and Google Analytics 4.
  • Tabby and Tamara buy-now-pay-later gateways.
  • Salesforce customer-support chat inside the app.
  • CMS-driven banners, so marketing could change promotions without an App Store submission.

Technical decisions

Put campaign content behind the CMS

The highest-leverage change was not a feature. Moving banners and promotional content to a CMS-driven model removed the App Store from the marketing critical path. A campaign could go live on the brand's schedule, not Apple's review queue.

One event vocabulary across three SDKs

Branch, MoEngage and GA4 each want to describe the same user action. Rather than let every screen call every SDK directly, the app defined the conversion events once and mapped them to each partner. That kept the 20+ event catalogue consistent and made it possible to add an event without touching three call sites.

Release discipline as a feature

Shipping 20+ production releases with zero critical post-release bugs is not luck; it is a process. Small, reviewable changes, a repeatable release checklist and a short window between "works on my machine" and "works in the store" are what made that number possible.

Results

  • 20+ production releases with zero critical post-release bugs.
  • 20+ conversion events tracked across three analytics partners.
  • Marketing promotions shipped through the CMS — no App Store submission required.
  • BNPL, support chat and in-store experiences delivered in one app.
  • Led a team of three iOS developers.

What I'd take from this: the most valuable release-engineering work is often invisible to users. Removing a dependency on the review queue changed what the business could do, not just how fast the team could ship.
